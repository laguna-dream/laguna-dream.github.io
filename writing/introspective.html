I really wanted to do 'fictober' (I kind of just made the term up, didn't realise it's already an existing challenge for fanfic/flash fiction) but the last few days I haven't really been able to write. I still want to attempt to make it through the month, but to make up for the backlog of 6~ days, I've decided to publish this draft that's been sitting around for a while that has a bunch of personal writing from my notes app circa 2020-2021. It counts for me!

i was rummaging around in my documents folder and i came across a folder called 'introspective' and decided to peek inside. i organise my files every 2-3 months when i feel like things are piling up a bit too high, usually in a frenzied burst of motivation to make sure everything has a place... which is quite a task because i am a hoarder of all kinds of words, images, videos, open files, etc. so there end up being these vague categorisations and i forget why i'd named something 'introspective' until i decide to peek in.

it is a collection of old writings/ramblings i had salvaged from an older hard drive. not /that/ old, around 2021? right around when i graduated from college. anyway it was amusing going through them and thinking, huh, maybe not much has changed at all.

most of it was also written in the pandemic so it is tinged with a kind of characteristic sadness of that time, at least one that i felt very heavily.

this was when i still had my asus rog laptop (rip) and i would just clickity clack write everything in... notepad. so a lot of txt files.

on bangalore flowers

one day in late january the chilly bangalore morning will give way to balmy sunshine and the purple jacaranda tree outside my building will begin to show signs of bloom. to me, this means that we are nearing the end of another academic year, that avocados and mangos will soon be in season, and that my birthday is coming. i feel a bit melancholic when i see the flowers for they loom like harbingers of change, comforting in their familiarity, disconcerting in their symbolism. an year older. a shift. a final semester of college. three familiar seasons, before i can say it again - spring is here.

the aftermath of this spring -- see: ambling errands under peach and green skies, pink plumeria in hair, wildflowers in lapel. heaps of fallen flowers smeared on tarred streets, heady crushed bloom scented strolls beneath wilting petals and pleasantly violet twilight evenings through winding streets of a small green canopied settlement on the edge of the city -- encased in elderly houses of abandoned lots and looming shadows. these are the flowers of a land - roadside trees; jacaranda and the raintree and pink tabebuias and golden trumpets -- settlers from far away lands in this city - nevertheless seared softly into memories of march as our necks crane above and stare, if only for a moment, in childlike amazement at the floral galaxies in the sky -- hibiscus and jasmine and rose bushes that peek over mossy garden walls, ripe for plucking by flower thieves in the frosted early hours of the morning -- sturdy wildflowers; dandelions and calyptocarpus, daisies and creeping foxglove - lining cement pavements and sewage ducts.

on writing i guess

If I wrote every day maybe I would learn to string sentences together again. I would be able to communicate the only way I know how. I’ve forgotten how to do it. The words don’t fall out like they did. I don’t know if I have thoughts anymore. I don’t speak. I don’t always remember what it was like to be a kid anymore. The thrill of returning to something you wrote at a very specific time and immediately reminds you of what it was like to be there again, like when you remember how it felt to be in your body as a kid. My childhood is something I held onto for very long, and I will always be at odds with the distance growing between what I used to be and whatever it is that’s happening.

Now I don’t remember things, and I always remembered things. Maybe I’d be able to craft worlds to fall asleep in again, I’d remember every mundane thing that happened to me, like when I wrote almost every day from the ages of 8 to 15, my life recorded over 5 diaries. I began to understand this concept of a voice after reading Anne Frank’s Diary cover to cover as a 11 year old. It heavily influenced my writing style – I was allowed to write about these things, document every miniscule detail of my life.

I only stopped because my father read my diary. A violation of trust. I was embarrassed and upset that my inner world had impressed itself upon someone else, without consent, of all people, my father. Something died within me that day and was the end of a consistent writing ‘practice’. But anyway, maybe if I wrote again I’d remember how fun it was to use language to construct worlds, to literally create anything you wanted to. How good it felt to just dump and dump and dump, sometimes return to it, sometimes not

I got better at writing when I started writing fanfiction. I read all the books, movies, stories I came across and I loved day dreaming. The worlds and thoughts and ideas inside me were too much to just stay inside. I talked. I wrote. I read. I was obsessed with it all. I don’t know how to do it anymore. Sometimes the passion returns but it never stays for long. God I sound old. I miss being older when I was younger. This isn’t fun at all.

*Anyway if I could write every day, I think I might be able to fall into my body again. At the very least, I’ll remember that I was here. Today. And tomorrow. Leaving college and floating around and not knowing what to do and not sure if I should say yes or no to anyone and watching and reading everything I can get my hands on and not worrying about utility or value or and signing up for reflective writing courses and having focus issues and feeling worthless but also feeling very good sometimes and of course those times are when I'm building things or in nature and maybe architecting a life, I don’t know. Maybe I’d remember all this.

almaari

in 2019, i made this illustration for almaari, which was a queer art project on instagram where people would submit artwork/reflections on what their 'closet' looked like, reimagining it as a physical space of comfort usually.

it's funny how i feel like i was able to live this out in the year or so, staying in a little house by the sea with cats, music, and assorted trinkets. i also like the little writing blurb i sent in with it.

mapping an island is fun once you realise that you have nothing but time, fresh greens, animal solidarity, and the slippery crystal sea. you can spend warm turquoise days wandering around the verdant croissant cluster, answering to no one, a new nook each day, foraging for berries and mushrooms and stems, turning them into crisp, pulverized, macerated, fermented, honeyed orange lavender delights, making yours the strange twinkling trinkets left by those before you, who must have stayed in the same old white ruin whose architecture one cannot trace to any known civilization, as you find your way back to a sliver of clarity that tells you that you can't really pin down this teeming underbelly through any human theoretical model, anymore than you can permanently trace your dreams on the sand, surrounded by the scattered attempts of others left behind in hopes that someone somewhere might find them one day, and understand, if even a little, what they wanted to say, pulsating weird oddities of nature whose heartbeats couldn't contain themselves any longer, impulse carved into rotting diaries, defunct personal blogs, shell leached dyes, weathered sculptures, diseased film roll, decaying banjos, glitched out crt monitors, potion tinctures, the sea moistened loom.
.

. like many, i am fond of the ocean. it does not carry shame, is not confounded by the mysterious and jagged, and will not falter in front of humans who recite to it awkward poetry, stirred by ancient melancholy, as their strained eyes settle on the horizon, and think, if even for a moment, well, this sure feels like a dream.
